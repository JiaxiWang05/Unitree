G1 EDU+ Mic and Camera Guide
Open Day robot greeter · procurement check · prices seen 28–31 Oct 2026 · updated 2 Nov

Microphones and cameras for the Unitree G1 EDU+ (23 DoF)

Which webcam and microphone to buy for our G1, and how to mount the webcam, checked against Unitree’s published port and power specs, the robot’s Linux system, and real user reports. Every item below is USB-powered at 5 V and plugs into the robot’s USB-C ports; nothing connects to the robot’s 12/24/58 V outputs.

What to buy

Camera

Logitech Brio 4K + 1 m USB-C to USB-C data cable (USB 3.x). About £83 on Amazon UK: Brio 4K for Business £69.99 (deal price) + CableCreation 1 m USB 3.2 Gen 2 £12.99. Backup if out of stock: Logitech C930e + USB-C (male) to USB-A (female) adapter. About £109. Our chest bracket is sized for the Brio, so a C930e would need the bracket’s camera size and screw position changed and the part reprinted.

Microphone

First: test the robot’s built-in mic Free. It has 4 mics with noise reduction; it only needs a mode setting (see below). If it is not good enough: Seeed ReSpeaker USB Mic Array (XVF-3000) + USB-C to Micro-B cable. About £58.

Small parts

Bracket, screws, ties Our 3D-printed chest bracket (PETG or ASA; see Mounting), 2× M6 screws with washers (probably M6×18 or M6×20), one ¼"-20 × 5/8" camera screw with two M6 washers, velcro cable ties, and a spare USB-C to USB-A adapter.

Recommended spend: about £83 (camera + 1 m cable) now, plus a few pounds of screws and filament, plus about £60 for the mic array only if the built-in mic fails the hall test. Buying the C930e as a second camera adds about £110 and removes the single-supplier risk.

Never plug anything into the XT30 power sockets. The robot’s panel has three XT30 sockets at 58 V (battery), 24 V and 12 V, each up to 5 A. A USB device connected there through a homemade lead would be destroyed. Webcams and mics go only into the USB-C ports, which supply 5 V.

The robot’s ports and power

All user ports are on one panel at the back of the neck (Unitree calls it the shoulder interface). They connect to PC2, the Jetson Orin NX computer our program runs on. Source: Unitree About G1 and help centre article 031347.

The built-in microphone: where it is and how to use it

Where

Four small holes in a row at the front of the neck, just above the chest plate: a 4-microphone linear array, 20 mm spacing, wired to PC1 (the motion computer). Unitree advises speaking from directly in front, within about 1 m. The speaker (5 W) is in the centre of the upper chest.

Sources: VuiClient Service component drawing; topology in help article 031345.

How to turn it on

  1. Put the voice assistant in wake-up mode: on the remote press L1 + L2 together, or in the Unitree app go to Device → Data → Audio → Voice assistant. Confirm the mode in the app; there is also a “closed” state.
  2. In the app, Device → Service Status, switch off chat_go, so Unitree’s own assistant does not answer visitors who say its wake word (“Hello Robot”).
  3. Our program then receives the noise-reduced audio over the robot’s internal network: multicast 239.168.123.161:5555, mono, 16 kHz, 16-bit, 5120-byte packets.
  • There is no physical button on the robot. Older notes (including ours) said “push-button mode”; Unitree documents wake-up mode for this stream, and users report silent or all-zero audio otherwise. Our software now says this and warns when the audio arrives all zeros.
  • Check it works: if the received audio is all zeros, the mode is wrong. Our program’s self-check reports the bytes it receives, and scripts/record_mic.py saves 5 seconds as mic_test.wav so you can listen to it.
  • If voice was never used on this robot and its firmware is 1.5.4 or later, Unitree says voice may need reactivating: with the robot online over Wi-Fi, press L1+L2+R1+R2+START on the remote, then reboot (help article 011335).
  • Unitree’s brochure claims echo cancellation for the array, but the developer docs only say “noise-reduced”. We keep the robot from listening while it speaks either way.

Cameras

Needs: a standard USB video (UVC) camera that works on Linux without a driver, gives 640×480 compressed video (MJPG) at 30 frames per second, lets software lock exposure and white balance, and has a wide view (about 90°) so people 1–5 m away stay in frame. The robot’s own head camera points 47.6° down at the floor, which is why we add one.

Mounting: on the chest, not the head

Unitree’s 3D-printable head brackets (xr_teleoperate Device.md; “Classic Head (98 mm)” and “Renewed Head (88 mm)” are the spacings of their two M4 screw holes) put a camera in the top of the face opening. That is the window the depth camera looks through to check the talk spot, and the Brio (102 mm wide) does not fit those brackets anyway.

So we designed a 3D-printed bracket that bolts to the two lower front M6 accessory holes on the chest (64.5 mm apart on Unitree’s About G1 drawing) and holds the Brio itself: its clip pulls off, and the ¼" tripod thread is under the camera body, so one screw from below holds it on a seat tilted 20° up. The thread sits about 14.5 mm in front of the camera’s back face and is shallow (a thin steel plate held in with tape), so the screw goes only 4 mm into it. The USB-C socket is low on the back face, so the back stops have a gap for the plug, and the shelf is open behind the camera so the stiff cable can bend up towards the shoulder. The lens ends up about 0.98 m above the floor. The tilt keeps a visitor’s face in view from about 0.75 m away, while people 3–5 m away are still seen from the thighs up. Checked against Unitree’s 3D model of the robot (unitree_ros): both pads sit flat, the rest of the bracket stays at least 3.3 mm off the chest, nothing blocks the webcam’s view, and none of the depth camera’s sight lines to the talk spot is blocked (the closest passes 15 mm from the camera). The camera’s size, thread and socket positions come from Logitech’s documents, teardown photos and published 3D-printed holders made around the real camera. The cable runs over the shoulder to the neck panel. Files are in the project’s hardware/brio_chest_mount folder (STL to print, STEP for CAD, parametric source).

Not yet fitted: before the final print, measure the hole spacing and thread depth on the robot, check that the Brio’s thread is 11.5–17.5 mm from its back face once the clip is off, and try a quick test print in cheap PLA (the final part is PETG or ASA). When fitting, the camera screw should turn about three full turns before it stops.

Why we cannot promise 100%, and how to close the gap

Unitree publishes the port voltages and currents, but not everything: whether the three USB-C ports share one power budget, how they map to the computer inside, or a load limit for the accessory holes. Manufacturers also rarely state Linux support for webcams and mics. The checks above reduce the risk as far as documents allow; the rest has to be confirmed on the robot. So:

  • Buy from sellers that accept returns (OneDirect, CPC, The Pi Hut, Logitech UK, Amazon UK), and test on the robot the day the items arrive.
  • Before ordering, confirm the robot’s identity: count the forearm joints (Unitree’s way to tell the 23-DoF and 29-DoF versions apart), read the label in the battery bay, and check that the depth camera’s cable runs from the back of the neck into a USB-C port (EDU+).

Camera test on arrival (on the robot’s computer)

lsusb -t
ls -l /dev/v4l/by-id/
v4l2-ctl -d /dev/v4l/by-id/<camera>-video-index0 --list-formats-ext
v4l2-ctl -d /dev/v4l/by-id/<camera>-video-index0 --list-ctrls-menus
v4l2-ctl -d /dev/v4l/by-id/<camera>-video-index0 \
  --set-fmt-video=width=640,height=480,pixelformat=MJPG \
  --stream-mmap --stream-count=300

Pass: MJPG 640×480 at 30 fps listed; exposure, white-balance and focus controls listed; the 300-frame stream runs at about 30 fps.

Microphone test on arrival

cat /proc/asound/cards
arecord -l
arecord -D plughw:CARD=<name>,DEV=0 -r 16000 -c 1 \
  -f S16_LE -d 10 test.wav
aplay test.wav   # or copy it to a laptop

Pass: the device is listed, records 10 seconds at 16 kHz, and the recording is clear. Repeat after a reboot with the mic left plugged in.

Note on the model name “G1 EDU+ 23 DoF”

Unitree’s own spec tables list only “G1” and “G1-EDU”. In Unitree’s support documents “EDU+” means the current EDU build with the depth camera wired to the user computer, which matches a 23-DoF robot. Some UK resellers instead use “EDU Plus” for the 29-DoF version, so if the purchase paperwork says “EDU Plus”, check the DoF. It is not the newer G1+ (launched September 2026), which has a moving head, different cameras, a 6-mic array and a different port panel; none of the above would apply to that robot.
